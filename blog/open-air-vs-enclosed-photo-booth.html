Open-Air vs Enclosed Photo Booth: Which Is Right for You?

Updated November 10, 2026 · By Leo Yeung, Owner of Handsome Photobooth

Quick answer: An open-air photo booth uses a styled backdrop, DSLR camera, and studio lighting in an open space—making it ideal for large wedding groups and modern reception decor. An enclosed photo booth uses curtains or walls that two to four guests step inside for a private, retro booth feel.

When you start shopping for a wedding photo booth, one of the first choices you will run into is open-air versus enclosed. They sound similar, but they create very different experiences for your guests and very different photos for your album. Below is how they compare so you can pick the right style for your Bay Area wedding or event.

What Is an Open-Air Photo Booth?

An open-air photo booth places a professional camera and lighting in front of a styled backdrop. There are no walls, so guests pose in an open area that anyone can see. Many modern weddings choose this style because it looks clean, fits elegant venues, and photographs well with a custom backdrop.

Because there is no enclosure, an open-air booth can fit large groups in a single shot. Bridal parties, families, and big friend groups can all squeeze into one photo, which is hard to do in a small enclosed space.

What Is an Enclosed Photo Booth?

An enclosed photo booth is the classic style many people picture first: a structure with curtains or walls that guests step inside. It feels private and a little playful, which can encourage guests to be sillier than they might be out in the open.

The tradeoff is space. Enclosed booths usually fit two to four people comfortably, so they are not ideal when you want big group shots. They also take up a fixed footprint that can feel bulky in smaller or more upscale venues.

Open-Air vs Enclosed: Which Costs More?

Pricing usually comes down to the equipment and what is included rather than the booth style alone. A modern open-air booth pairs a DSLR camera, studio lighting, and a custom backdrop, while enclosed booths vary from older self-service units to staffed print booths.

When you compare quotes, look at the full package: setup and breakdown, an on-site attendant, unlimited prints, digital or QR downloads, props, and a custom photo template. A booth that looks cheaper up front can end up costing more once those extras are added back in. Which One Should You Choose?

For most Bay Area weddings, an open-air photo booth is the more versatile fit. It handles big group shots, matches modern decor, and the backdrop becomes part of your reception styling. If your guest list leans toward large families and bridal parties, open-air is usually the right call.

An enclosed booth can be a good fit for casual events, birthday parties, or retro-themed celebrations where small groups of two to four guests want a private, curtain-closed experience and the venue has room for the enclosure.

How Much Space Does Each Booth Need?

Plan for approximately 10 ft × 10 ft for an open-air booth so there is room for the backdrop, camera, lighting, and prop table. An enclosed booth needs a similar footprint plus clearance for the walls or curtains. Either way, the booth should sit near a standard power outlet and stay close enough to the action that guests actually use it.

Open-Air vs Enclosed Photo Booth FAQs

What is the difference between an open-air and enclosed photo booth?

An open-air photo booth uses a backdrop and a camera on a stand, so guests pose in an open space with no walls. An enclosed photo booth is a curtained or walled structure that guests step inside. Open-air booths fit larger groups and match modern wedding decor, while enclosed booths offer more privacy for silly, candid photos.

Which photo booth is better for a wedding?

Most Bay Area weddings choose an open-air photo booth because it handles large groups, photographs well with a custom backdrop, and fits smoothly into reception layouts without bulky walls. Enclosed booths suit casual or retro-themed events where small groups of two to four guests want extra privacy.

How much space does a photo booth need?

An open-air photo booth typically needs about 10 ft × 10 ft for the backdrop, camera, and guests. An enclosed booth needs a similar footprint plus clearance for the structure. Both require access to a standard power outlet nearby.
